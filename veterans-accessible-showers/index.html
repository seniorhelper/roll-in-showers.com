Accessible Showers for Veterans: VA Program Planning

VA has three programs that can help pay for a roll-in shower. Which one fits depends on the veteran's disability rating and whether the condition is service-connected. The order of steps matters, because VA approval comes before construction.

Quick answer

Which VA program fits

HISA (Home Improvements and Structural Alterations) is a VA health care benefit for medically necessary changes, such as a roll-in shower, for veterans enrolled in VA health care. SAH (Specially Adapted Housing) and SHA (Special Home Adaptation) are larger housing grants for veterans with specific service-connected disabilities. Eligible veterans may qualify for more than one.

This page summarizes public VA information. VA decides eligibility, and amounts change. Confirm everything with VA before signing a contract.

HISA at a glance

Details from VA sources
WhoVeterans and service members enrolled in VA health care, with a VA provider's prescription for the improvement
Lifetime limit (as listed by VA)$6,800 for a service-connected condition, or for a veteran rated 50% or more service-connected; $2,000 for other enrolled veterans
Typical usesAccess to the home and to essential bathroom facilities, such as a roll-in shower, along with doorway widening and similar changes
How it paysThrough the VA prosthetics office after approval; follow its process for bids and payment
Pending changesH.R. 2245 (119th Congress) proposes higher limits; check its status and VA's page for current amounts

The HISA limit is lifetime, not per project, so a veteran who used part of it earlier has less available. Ask the prosthetics office for the remaining balance.

02

SAH and SHA grants

Specially Adapted Housing and Special Home Adaptation grants are for veterans with certain severe service-connected disabilities, such as the loss of use of limbs or specific vision or respiratory conditions. They can fund much larger home adaptations, including building or remodeling a bathroom.

For fiscal year 2026 (October 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026), VA's Federal Register notice set the SAH maximum at $126,526 and the SHA maximum at $25,349. VA adjusts these amounts each October 1, so check the VA housing grants page for the current year.

03

The order of steps

  • Talk with the veteran's VA provider about the need; ask for an evaluation by an occupational or physical therapist if helpful.
  • Get the prescription or referral the program requires.
  • Contact the VA prosthetics office (for HISA) or VA housing grants (for SAH and SHA) and ask for the application packet and bid requirements.
  • Collect the bids the program asks for, using the same written scope for each contractor.
  • Wait for written approval before any work begins. Work done before approval may not be paid.
  • Complete the work, keep before-and-after photos and the final invoice, and submit what VA asks for.
Common mistake

Starting demolition before VA approves the project. Ask the program office what approval looks like and get it in writing.

04

Writing a scope VA can approve

A clear scope helps VA reviewers and makes bids comparable. Describe the medical need in plain terms ("veteran uses a manual wheelchair and cannot step over a tub wall"), then the work: remove tub, install roll-in pan of a stated size with a low or flush threshold, hand shower on a 59-inch or longer hose, grab bars with blocking, folding seat, and any door widening. Our dimensions guide and ADA reference help you specify sizes.

05

Other help for veterans

Some states and counties run veteran home modification programs, and veteran service organizations can help with paperwork. Your county veterans service officer is a good first call. Nonprofit programs may also help where VA amounts do not cover the full project.

Common questions

Veterans program questions

How much does the VA HISA grant pay?

VA has listed a lifetime HISA limit of $6,800 for veterans with service-connected conditions or a rating of 50 percent or more, and $2,000 for other enrolled veterans. Check VA for current amounts.

Can HISA pay for a roll-in shower?

HISA can pay for medically necessary improvements such as access to bathroom facilities, which can include a roll-in shower, when prescribed and approved by VA.

What is the SAH grant amount for 2026?

VA's notice for fiscal year 2026 set the Specially Adapted Housing maximum at $126,526 and the Special Home Adaptation maximum at $25,349. Amounts are adjusted each October 1.

Should I start work before VA approves it?

No. Get written approval first. Work started before approval may not be paid by the program.
