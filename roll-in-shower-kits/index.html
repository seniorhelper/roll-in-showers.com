Roll-In Shower Kits: What You Get and What You Still Need

A roll-in shower kit usually pairs a low-threshold base with matching wall panels. It can save weeks of tile work, but it rarely includes everything a finished accessible shower needs. Here is how to read a kit before you buy one.

Quick answer

What "kit" usually means

In this category, a kit or package is a shower base designed for wheelchair or low-step entry plus a wall system made to fit it, often in several pieces. Some packages add integrated soap shelves, a drain, or a seat. Plumbing valves, the hand shower, grab bars, blocking and installation are usually separate.

Kits suit tub replacements where the alcove matches a stock size. Custom tile suits odd-shaped rooms or a fully recessed curbless floor. Pan-only purchases sit in between: a molded base with whatever walls the installer prefers.

02

Multi-piece vs one-piece

One-piece shower units are molded as a single shell. They have no wall seams, but a 60-inch one-piece unit generally cannot fit through a standard interior door, so it is mainly used in new construction before walls are framed. Multi-piece kits break the walls into panels that can be carried up stairs and through doorways, which is why most remodels use them. Three of the six packages linked from this site are named multi-piece in their listing titles.

03

What a kit usually leaves out

  • Shower valve and trim (often reused or replaced separately)
  • Hand shower, slide bar and a hose of at least 59 inches
  • Grab bars and the blocking or anchors behind them
  • A folding seat, unless the listing says it is included
  • Curtain rod, weighted curtain, or glass panel
  • Drain body, if the listing shows only the grate
  • Backer board or waterproofing behind panels, if the manufacturer requires it
  • Adhesives, sealants and trim pieces
  • Freight, removal of the old tub and disposal

Ask the seller for the packing list and installation manual before ordering. The manual tells you what the manufacturer expects to be behind the panels, which affects labor cost.

04

Checking fit before you order

Compare the kit's specification sheet to your measurements, not to the size in the product name. Confirm the finished inside dimensions, the rough opening the walls need, the drain location, the threshold height at the entry, and whether the base is left- or right-hand. For roll-in use, also confirm that the threshold and any built-in curb are low enough for the user's chair. Our dimensions guide has a measuring routine.

05

Delivery and inspection

Packages often ship by freight on a pallet. Plan where it will be unloaded and stored, and inspect every carton before signing. Note damage on the delivery receipt, take photos, and report it within the carrier's or seller's deadline. Keep the panels flat and protected until installation day.

06

Who installs a kit

A handy homeowner can sometimes set panels, but the base, drain connection, valve and blocking are where most leaks and loose bars start. Many people buy the kit and hire the plumbing and base work. Common questions

Kit questions

What comes in a roll-in shower kit?

Usually a low-threshold shower base and a matching wall system, sometimes with shelves or a drain. Valves, hand showers, grab bars, seats and installation are usually separate. Check the packing list.

Are multi-piece shower kits good for remodels?

Yes. Multi-piece walls can be carried through doors and up stairs, which a one-piece 60-inch unit generally cannot, so they are the usual choice for remodels.

Can I install a roll-in shower kit myself?

Some homeowners set the panels, but the base, drain, valve and grab bar blocking need careful work and sometimes a permit. Many people hire those parts out.

How do I know a kit will fit my bathroom?

Compare the specification sheet to your field measurements: finished inside size, rough opening, drain position, threshold height and the side of the entry.
