Roll-In vs Walk-In Shower: The Important Differences

"Walk-in" and "roll-in" are often used as if they meant the same thing. They do not. The difference is whether a wheel can cross the entry, and that changes the floor, the size and the drain.

Quick answer

One-line definitions

A walk-in shower is any shower you step into without climbing over a tub wall. It usually has a curb a few inches high. A roll-in shower has no curb, or a beveled lip of ½ inch or less, and is large enough for a shower wheelchair, with 30 × 60 inches inside as the common ADA reference.

Terms you will see in listings

  • Barrier-free: no curb, or a very low beveled lip. Describes the entry, not the size.
  • Zero-entry / curbless: fully flush entry.
  • Low-threshold: a small lip; check the actual height.
  • Transfer shower: a 36 × 36 stall used from a seat.
  • "ADA shower": a marketing term unless the installed room meets the ADA Standards in a setting they cover.
  • Step-in: implies a curb that is stepped over.
03

Who each one suits

Walk-in showers suit people who walk steadily and want an easier entry than a tub. Roll-in showers suit people who use wheelchairs or rolling shower chairs, people who need a caregiver's help, and households planning for the long term. A curbless shower that is too small for a chair gives the look of a roll-in without the function, so size matters as much as the entry.

04

Cost drivers

A walk-in shower with a curb can sit on the existing floor. A roll-in usually needs the floor adjusted so it can slope to the drain without a curb, plus waterproofing past the entry. That floor work is the main reason roll-ins cost more. See roll-in shower cost for published ranges.

05

Can one shower do both?

Yes. A curbless shower at least 60 inches long, with blocking for bars and a seat, works as a walk-in today and a roll-in later. That is often the best choice for a remodel that has to last.

Common questions

Comparison questions

What is the difference between a roll-in and walk-in shower?

A walk-in shower usually has a low curb you step over. A roll-in shower has no curb, or a lip of ½ inch or less, and is big enough for a shower wheelchair.

Is a curbless shower a roll-in shower?

Only if it is large enough for a wheelchair user. Curbless describes the entry; roll-in describes both the entry and the size.

Which costs more?

Roll-in showers usually cost more because the floor often has to be recessed or specially built so it can slope without a curb.
