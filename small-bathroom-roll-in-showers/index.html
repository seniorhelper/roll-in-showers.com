Making a compact room work

Roll-In Showers for Small Bathrooms: Layout Strategies

Many American bathrooms are about 5 by 8 feet, with a tub along the back wall. That room can hold a roll-in shower if the clear spaces overlap and the door, sink and toilet are arranged around the wheelchair.

Quick answer

The core idea

In a small accessible bathroom, the same open floor does several jobs: it is the space in front of the shower, in front of the toilet and the sink, and the turning space. Fixtures that project into it (a vanity cabinet, a swinging door, glass) are what make a small room fail. Remove those projections and the room works.

In one sentenceOverlap the clear spaces and remove anything that projects into them.Measure your room →
01

Strategies that free up floor

  • Swap an inward-swinging door for an outward swing, a pocket door or a barn-style slider.
  • Replace a vanity cabinet with a wall-hung sink that leaves knee space.
  • Use a curtain instead of glass at the shower entry.
  • Choose a shower the full width of the room so its entry clearance doubles as the room's main floor.
  • Mount the toilet paper holder and towel bars where they do not block grab bars.
  • Consider a wall-hung or compact toilet if it lets the clear space work.
02

A typical 5 × 8 conversion

In a classic 5 × 8 bathroom with a 60-inch tub across the back, replacing the tub with a 60 × 30 roll-in pan leaves the rest of the room for the toilet, sink and approach. The ADA clear space in front of a standard roll-in is 30 by 60 inches, and a lavatory is allowed at the end of it opposite the shower controls, which is exactly how these rooms are laid out. The open floor in front of the shower is also where a wheelchair turns, if the room is deep enough.

Whether a full 60-inch turning circle fits depends on the actual room. If it does not, the user may back in or out, which many manage fine with practice.

03

Corner and neo-angle options

If the bathroom has no tub alcove, a corner pan can tuck into an unused corner. Neo-angle pans save floor space but have a narrower, diagonal entry that is harder for a wheelchair. For a wheelchair user, a rectangular corner pan with the open side facing the room usually works better than a neo-angle.

04

Wet-room approach

Some small bathrooms are built as wet rooms: the whole floor is waterproofed and slopes to a drain, and the shower is defined only by a curtain. This gives the most usable floor but needs careful waterproofing of the entire room and a toilet and vanity that tolerate splash.

05

What to measure first

Measure the room's inside dimensions, the door's clear width and swing, the toilet centerline from the side wall, the sink projection, and where the drain and window are. Then tape the shower and clear spaces on the floor and try the route with the actual chair before committing.

Common questions

Small bathroom questions

Can you put a roll-in shower in a 5 by 8 bathroom?

Often, by replacing the tub with a 60-inch roll-in pan and keeping the floor in front of it clear. Door swing and vanity choices usually decide whether it works.

What is the smallest roll-in shower?

The ADA standard roll-in reference is 30 by 60 inches inside. Smaller curbless showers exist but may not hold a shower wheelchair.

Should a small accessible bathroom have a glass shower door?

Usually not across the entry, because it blocks the chair and takes floor space. A curtain keeps the opening clear.
