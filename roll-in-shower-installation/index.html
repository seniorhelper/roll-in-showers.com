Roll-In Shower Installation: Process, Trades and Timeline

A roll-in shower installation is a short sequence of trades: demolition, carpentry, plumbing, waterproofing, setting the base and walls, then fixtures and safety hardware. Knowing the order helps you ask the right questions at each step.

Quick answer

How long it takes

A like-for-like swap, where a tub comes out and a molded roll-in pan with panel walls goes into the same alcove with the drain in place, is often finished in one day. Aging Safely Baths installs walk-in tubs and roll-in showers in one day. Projects that recess a floor, move a drain under a slab, widen a door or use tile take longer, plus time for any permit and inspection.

01

1. Assessment and measuring

The installer measures the room, the opening, the drain, the route and the user's needs, and looks at the floor structure from below if possible. This is when the product and the type of curbless entry are chosen. Ask for a written scope that names the product, the drain plan and what happens if hidden damage is found.

02

2. Permits and ordering

Many jurisdictions require a permit for plumbing changes, and some for any shower replacement. Ask who pulls the permit and whether an inspection is needed before walls close. Products are ordered against the final measurements; freight items are inspected on arrival.

03

3. Protection and demolition

Floors and the route to the door are covered. The water is shut off, the old tub or shower and surrounding wall material are removed, and the debris is hauled away. Demolition reveals what is behind the walls, so this is the point where a change order is most likely. A good installer stops and shows you the issue before continuing.

04

4. Floor, framing and blocking

The subfloor is repaired or leveled. For a curbless entry, the shower area may be recessed. Blocking (solid wood or approved backing) goes in for every grab bar, the seat and the slide bar, ideally covering a wider zone than the planned locations so bars can be added later. Take photos with a tape measure showing where the blocking is.

05

5. Plumbing

The drain is adapted or moved to match the pan, the valve is replaced or repositioned within reach of the seat, and a supply is added for a hand shower if needed. A pressure-balance or thermostatic valve limits scalding. This stage is where a plumbing inspection often happens.

06

6. Base, waterproofing and walls

The pan is set on a full bed of the specified setting material, leveled, and connected to the drain. Waterproofing goes on according to the wall system's instructions, extending past the entry for curbless designs. Then the wall panels or tile go up. For tile showers, a flood test before tile is good practice.

07

7. Fixtures, bars and seat

Trim, the hand shower and slide bar, grab bars, the folding seat and the curtain rod are installed. Bars are fastened into the blocking and tested by pulling firmly. Controls are checked for reach from the seat.

08

8. Testing and handoff

  • Run the shower and check that water drains without reaching the entry.
  • Check for leaks at the drain and valve.
  • Have the user try the entry, transfer and controls, with a helper present.
  • Collect product manuals, warranty registrations, blocking photos and the final invoice.
  • Ask what cleaners are safe for the pan and walls, and when caulk should be checked.

Common questions

Installation questions

How long does a roll-in shower installation take?

A tub-to-roll-in swap with a molded pan and panel walls and no drain move is often done in one day. Floor recessing, slab cutting, tile or door widening add days, plus any permit inspections.

Do I need a permit for a roll-in shower?

Often, especially when plumbing or structure changes. Rules vary by city and county, so ask the installer who will pull the permit.

What is blocking and why does it matter?

Blocking is solid backing inside the wall that grab bars, seats and slide bars screw into. Without it, bars can pull out under body weight.

Can I use the shower the same day?

Ask the installer. Some adhesives and sealants need curing time before the shower is used; follow the manufacturer's instructions.
