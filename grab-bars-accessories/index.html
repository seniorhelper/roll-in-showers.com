Hardware that makes the shower usable

Roll-In Shower Grab Bars, Controls and Accessories

The shower footprint gets the user in; the hardware makes it usable. Grab bars, the hand shower, the valve and storage all have to be reachable from where the person sits.

Quick answer

The hardware checklist

A complete roll-in shower usually has: horizontal grab bars on solid blocking, a hand shower on a slide bar with a hose at least 59 inches long, a pressure-balance or thermostatic valve with a lever handle, a weighted curtain on a rod that clears the entry, and storage between 15 and 48 inches high.

Grab bar sizes and heights

Wall clearance1½ in. between bar and wallRoom for the hand without trapping the arm
StrengthResist 250 lb of force with fastenersBody weight plus a sudden load

Bars should have a textured or knurled grip that holds when wet. Colors that contrast with the wall help people with low vision find the bar.

02

Where bars go in a roll-in shower

With a seat, bars go on the back wall and on the side wall opposite the seat, but not above the seat. Without a seat, bars go on all three walls. Many homes add a vertical bar at the entry, which is not part of the ADA layout but helps people stand up or step in. An occupational therapist can mark positions for the specific user.

03

Hand showers and slide bars

A hand shower on a slide bar lets the spray height change for seated and standing users and lets a caregiver hold it. The ADA requires a hose of at least 59 inches and a spray that can be used fixed or hand-held, with an on/off control. Some slide bars are rated as grab bars; most are not, so do not let anyone use a standard slide bar for support.

04

Valves, controls and water temperature

Lever handles are easier than knobs for people with arthritis or limited grip. A pressure-balance or thermostatic valve limits temperature swings and scalding. The ADA caps shower water at 120°F; at home, set the anti-scald limit on the valve. Place the valve where it can be reached from the seat or chair before the water hits the person, which in a roll-in is usually on the back wall close to the seat end.

05

Curtains, retainers and storage

  • Weighted-hem curtains stay put without blocking the entry.
  • A curved or straight rod should clear the head of a person in a shower chair being pushed in.
  • Collapsible rubber water retainers fold flat under wheels.
  • Recessed niches or shelves between 15 and 48 inches high can be reached seated.
  • A hook for a towel and robe just outside the spray.
06

Hardware to avoid

  • Suction-cup "grab bars" for support or transfers
  • Towel bars used as grab bars
  • Glass doors across a roll-in entry
  • Knob-style valves that need a firm twist
  • Bath mats with curled edges

Common questions

Hardware questions

How high should grab bars be in a shower?

The ADA reference is 33 to 36 inches above the floor, measured to the top of the bar. A therapist can adjust positions for the individual user.

What diameter grab bar is best?

The ADA allows 1¼ to 2 inches round. Most people find 1¼ or 1½ inches easiest to grip.

How long should a hand shower hose be?

At least 59 inches, per the ADA Standards. Longer hoses help caregivers.

Can grab bars go on shower wall panels?

Only with blocking behind the panel or anchors approved by the panel and bar makers for that wall. Plan blocking before the panels go up.
