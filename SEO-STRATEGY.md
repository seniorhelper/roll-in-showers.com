# Roll-In-Showers.com Rebuild Strategy

## Goal

Build the strongest useful answer for people researching, planning, specifying, buying, or installing a roll-in shower. The homepage targets the broad phrase “roll in showers” while helping visitors self-select into product, planning, accessibility, cost, or installation paths.

## Content architecture

- Commercial hub: homepage, kits, pans, costs, installation, tub conversion.
- Accessibility cluster: wheelchair showers, barrier-free showers, ADA showers, dimensions, senior showers, seats, grab bars.
- Planning cluster: small bathrooms, roll-in vs. walk-in, DIY, design ideas.
- Funding and trust cluster: veterans, funding, about.
- Editorial cluster: blog gateway plus four deeply researched practical guides.

Each substantive page has one primary intent. Exact-match phrasing is used only where natural. Related language, questions, entities, measurements, decision factors, and product terminology provide topical depth without keyword repetition.

## Internal linking

- Every page links upward to the homepage and laterally to 3–6 genuinely relevant next steps.
- Commercial pages link to Showers4Less product detail pages only where a product helps the reader act.
- Accessibility pages cite primary government sources and link to related planning pages.
- Blog posts feed authority into the evergreen guide most closely matching the article.
- Breadcrumbs, contextual links, related-guide cards, HTML sitemap navigation, and XML sitemap work together without sitewide keyword-heavy footer links.

## Evidence and claims policy

- ADA dimensions come from ADA.gov or the U.S. Access Board.
- VA and Medicare topics link to the administering agency and avoid promises of eligibility or reimbursement.
- Prices are planning ranges, not quotes; variables and update dates are shown.
- “ADA compliant” is not used as a blanket promise for a private residence or product by itself.
- No invented testimonials, project counts, certifications, awards, warranties, or installation coverage.
- Product links, dimensions, and images come from the supplied Showers4Less source. Buyers are instructed to verify the live product page before ordering.

## Design direction

“Architectural confidence”: deep navy, vivid aqua, warm copper, crisp white, technical line drawings, dimensional callouts, layered cards, and subtle motion. It should feel more like a premium accessible-bath design studio than a generic senior-services template.

## Conversion path

1. Understand the difference between roll-in, walk-in, barrier-free, and ADA shower terminology.
2. Choose a planning path: shop kits/pans, estimate a conversion, or request guidance.
3. Use the existing toll-free number or quote form.
4. Product-specific “View details / Buy at Showers4Less” buttons open the supplied store in a new tab.

## Technical plan

- Static, crawlable HTML on every route.
- One H1, descriptive titles and meta descriptions, canonicals, Open Graph tags, breadcrumbs, FAQ markup, and Article/WebPage schema as appropriate.
- Organization/WebSite schema identifies the publisher without inventing reviews.
- Responsive navigation, skip link, keyboard-visible focus, reduced-motion support, semantic landmarks, descriptive alt text, and large touch targets.
- robots.txt, sitemap.xml, llms.txt, humans.txt, web app manifest, favicon, and social share SVG.
- Shared minified-light CSS and JavaScript; no framework dependency.
