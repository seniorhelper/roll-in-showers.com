Funding an Accessible Bathroom: Programs and Questions

No single program pays for every accessible shower, and rules change. This page lists the programs people most often ask about, what each one actually says, and how to check whether you qualify.

Quick answer

The honest overview

Original Medicare generally does not pay for a bathroom remodel; it covers durable medical equipment such as commode chairs when a provider orders them. Medicaid home and community-based services waivers in many states can fund home modifications for eligible people. Veterans may qualify for VA HISA, SAH or SHA grants. USDA offers repair loans and grants for low-income rural homeowners. Your local Area Agency on Aging knows the local programs. And IRS Publication 502 explains when the cost may count as a medical expense.

None of these are guarantees. Eligibility depends on the program's rules, and many require approval before work starts.

01

Medicare: what it covers and what it does not

Original Medicare Part B covers durable medical equipment (DME) that a doctor or other provider orders for use in the home, from a supplier enrolled in Medicare. Medicare.gov lists commode chairs among covered items. A remodel, a new shower pan, tile or permanently installed grab bars are not DME, so Original Medicare generally does not pay for them.

Some Medicare Advantage plans offer supplemental benefits that can include bathroom safety items, under CMS guidance that lets plans cover "primarily health related" benefits. These vary by plan and year. Call the plan and ask what is covered, what documentation is needed, and whether a specific vendor is required.

02

Medicaid waivers and state programs

Most states run Medicaid home and community-based services (HCBS) waivers that help people who would otherwise need nursing-home-level care stay at home. Many waivers include "home modifications" or "environmental accessibility adaptations," which can include accessible showers. Each state sets its own limits, eligibility rules and approved contractor lists, and waivers often have waiting lists. Start with your state Medicaid agency or Area Agency on Aging.

03

Area Agencies on Aging

Area Agencies on Aging coordinate local services for older adults, and many run or refer to home repair and modification programs. The national Eldercare Locator, run by the U.S. Administration for Community Living, connects you with your local agency at eldercare.acl.gov or 1-800-677-1116. Ask about home modification grants, nonprofit repair programs and any local funding cycles.

04

Veterans: HISA, SAH and SHA

VA's Home Improvements and Structural Alterations (HISA) benefit helps eligible veterans make medically necessary changes, including bathroom access such as roll-in showers. VA has listed lifetime limits of $6,800 for service-connected conditions (and for veterans rated 50 percent or more service-connected) and $2,000 for other veterans enrolled in VA health care. Larger Specially Adapted Housing (SAH) and Special Home Adaptation (SHA) grants serve veterans with qualifying service-connected disabilities. Details, current amounts and the order of steps are on our veterans page.

05

USDA Section 504 repair loans and grants

In eligible rural areas, USDA Rural Development's Section 504 program offers very-low-income homeowners repair loans (commonly listed up to $40,000 at 1 percent for 20 years) and, for homeowners 62 and older who cannot repay a loan, grants (commonly listed up to $10,000) to remove health and safety hazards. Accessibility changes can qualify. Check eligibility maps and current limits with your local USDA office.

06

Taxes: IRS Publication 502

IRS Publication 502 says capital expenses for medical care, including home improvements whose main purpose is medical care, can be included in medical expenses. The publication lists modifications such as installing railings, support bars or other modifications to bathrooms for a person with a disability, which generally do not increase home value and may be included in full. If an improvement does increase the home's value, only the cost above that increase counts. Medical expenses are deductible only if you itemize and only above 7.5 percent of adjusted gross income.

Keep the doctor's recommendation, the itemized invoice and any appraisal. Talk with a tax professional before you count on a deduction.

07

Questions to ask any program

  • Who is eligible, and what documentation is needed?
  • Does the program have to approve the work, the contractor or the bid before work starts?
  • Is there a dollar limit, and is it lifetime or per project?
  • Does the program pay the contractor, reimburse you, or pay part up front?
  • How long does approval usually take?
  • What happens if the final cost is higher than the approved amount?

Common questions

Funding questions

Does Medicare pay for a walk-in or roll-in shower?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for bathroom remodeling. It covers durable medical equipment such as commode chairs when a provider orders it. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer supplemental benefits for bathroom safety items, so check your plan.

Can Medicaid pay for an accessible shower?

In many states, Medicaid home and community-based services waivers include home modifications for eligible people. Rules, limits and waiting lists vary by state.

Is a roll-in shower tax deductible?

IRS Publication 502 allows some home modifications for a disability to count as medical expenses if you itemize, above 7.5 percent of adjusted gross income. Consult a tax professional about your situation.

Who can help me find local programs?

Your Area Agency on Aging. Find it through the Eldercare Locator at eldercare.acl.gov or 1-800-677-1116.
