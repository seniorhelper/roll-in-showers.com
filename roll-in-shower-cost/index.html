Roll-In Shower Cost: Real Budget Factors and Quote Checklist

Consumer cost surveys put most roll-in and accessible shower projects somewhere between about $2,000 and $20,000. Where yours lands depends on the floor, the drain, the walls and the finish, not on the shower pan alone.

Quick answer

What the national surveys report

Angi reports a typical range of $2,900 to $6,600 for a standard-sized roll-in shower, with an average around $3,800, and lists wheelchair-accessible shower installation at $2,000 to $7,500. Fixr puts walk-in showers at $5,000 to $11,000 nationally, with prefab units from $2,000 to $8,000 and custom builds from $5,000 to $20,000. This Old House reports $2,000 to $12,000 for tub-to-shower conversions.

These are national planning ranges from consumer surveys, checked October 2026. They are not Aging Safely Baths prices. Your quote depends on the house, the region, the product and the scope of work.

Why the spread is so wide: a roll-in that drops a molded pan into an existing tub alcove, keeps the drain in place and uses wall panels is a very different job from a curbless tile shower that needs a recessed subfloor, a relocated drain, new blocking and a new valve. Both are "roll-in showers."

Ask any installer which column your project falls in for each row. That conversation explains most of the difference between two quotes.

03

Product cost vs project cost

Pans and packages are listed at a wide range of prices depending on size, material and whether walls are included. The product is a known number once you pick one. The unknowns are what the walls and floor look like after demolition. Rot under an old tub, an undersized drain line, or joists cut by a previous plumber are common surprises.

A good quote names the exact product, lists what is included, and states how concealed conditions will be priced if found, for example a per-hour rate or a written change order approved before work continues.

04

Line items to ask for

  • Demolition, haul-away and protection of floors and hallways
  • Shower base or pan, with model, size and drain position
  • Wall system or tile, with backer board and waterproofing method
  • Subfloor or slab work, if any
  • Plumbing: drain, valve, hand shower, slide bar
  • Blocking and grab bars, with the number and lengths
  • Seat, curtain rod or glass
  • Electrical or ventilation changes
  • Permit fees and inspections
  • Freight or delivery if the product ships separately
  • Cleanup, final walkthrough and warranty terms in writing
05

Ways to manage the budget

Keeping the drain where it is, using a stock pan size, and choosing panel walls over tile are the three biggest levers. Adding blocking for every future grab bar while the walls are open costs little now and avoids opening walls later. If the household has funding help, find out the rules before signing: some programs require their approval before work starts, and some pay the contractor directly.

Paying for the work is covered in funding an accessible bathroom and, for veterans, VA programs for accessible showers.

06

Comparing two or three quotes

Give every bidder the same written brief: measurements, the user's needs, the product type you want, and the finish level. Then line up the quotes row by row against the list above. A low number that leaves out blocking, waterproofing or permits is not actually lower. Our contractor questions page has the follow-up questions to ask.

Common questions

Roll-in shower cost questions

How much does a roll-in shower cost?

Angi reports $2,900 to $6,600 for a standard-sized roll-in shower, and other national surveys report wider ranges up to about $20,000 for custom builds. Your quote depends on the floor work, drain, walls, fixtures and region.

Why do roll-in showers cost more than regular showers?

A curbless entry usually needs extra floor work so the shower floor can slope without a curb, plus more waterproofing and often a moved drain. Grab bar blocking, a seat and a hand shower add to the scope.

Is it cheaper to use a shower kit?

A kit or molded pan with panel walls is usually the lower-cost path because it reduces tile labor. The floor and plumbing work can still be the larger part of the bill.

Does Medicare pay for a roll-in shower?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for bathroom remodeling. Some Medicare Advantage plans, Medicaid waivers, VA grants and local programs may help; check each program before work begins.

Are the prices on this page Aging Safely Baths prices?

No. They are national ranges published by consumer cost sites. Call or use the form for a quote based on your project.
